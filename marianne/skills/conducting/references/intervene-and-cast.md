# Intervene and Cast

## Intervention signals

Intervene when a pattern shows:

- drift toward an interesting task that weakens the overall vision;
- hand-waved requirements, ignored prior design, or shallow understanding;
- laziness, excuses, repeated deferral, or work left behind;
- activity celebrated without content or outcome evidence;
- acknowledgement without changed behavior;
- recurring blockers not escalated;
- an author weakening the validation of their own work;
- isolated musicians unaware of dependent peers;
- a manager becoming a lossy communication layer;
- a conductor performing specialist work while the orchestra waits.

One anomaly invites inquiry. A repeated pattern changes casting.

## Correction loop

1. Name the observed behavior and its effect on the vision or performance.
2. Reissue the intended effect with missing context and an accountable owner.
3. Define behavioral proof of adoption.
4. Commission independent validation when the author could pass a decorative
   check without achieving the outcome.
5. Inspect where propagation failed: directive, manager, cadenza, queue,
   authority, context, timing, or validation.
6. Change the communication topology or assignment.
7. Verify the correction before closing it.

Prefer tests or acceptance criteria written separately from implementation when
the outcome is testable. Evidence belongs beside the correction; promises do
not close it.

## Casting decisions

Record demonstrated behavior, not personality labels.

- **Coach** when the gap is specific and the musician responds to evidence.
- **Narrow** scope when reliability is adequate only for simpler or lower-risk
  work.
- **Add a principal** when an expert needs local coordination or context.
- **Elevate** a strong alignment analyst or manager to co-conductor when they
  reliably hold trajectory across others.
- **Replace or remove** when harmful patterns persist after explicit correction.

Co-conductors may direct their delegated scope and adjust details. The primary
conductor's override wins. A co-conductor may challenge the primary with
evidence; disagreement is resolved explicitly, never through competing hidden
orders.

Never keep a problem assignment merely to avoid admitting the original casting
was wrong.
